\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

We ran 291 runs: 45 calibration (5 per condition), 90 main, 62 overhead,
70 intervention, 16 A/B and 8 receiver-placement runs. All passed the noise
gate. Median platform noise was \noiseMed\% of vCPU time.

\subsection{Measurement validity}
In all \valRuns{} traced runs every request received kernel features
(coverage \valCoverage\%), there were no slot collisions, and
\valDrops{} events were lost. The accounting identity closed within
5\,$\mu$s for at least \valWithin\% of requests in every run (median
residual 167\,ns, which is the distance between the timestamp and the slot
snapshot). Generator lateness was below 60\,$\mu$s at p99 in \genOk{} of
\genAll{} main runs; the worst run reached \genMax.

\textbf{Platform collapse episodes.} A minority of runs, traced or not,
contain episodes of a few hundred milliseconds in which the queue grows to
tens of milliseconds or more. These episodes make the shelves in
Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}. In the worst kv run, the median service time
doubled (22.5$\to$49.8\,$\mu$s) for 600\,ms with no scheduler, IRQ or
blocking events: on-CPU time simply got longer. That is consistent with
the host slowing the vCPU, which the guest cannot observe. Other episodes
show neither. A single episode in a calibration run raised its p99 to
\thrMaxCpuMid{} for cpu-mid, against a five-run median of \thrCpuMid{}, so
we use the median as the tail threshold.

\subsection{Overhead}
\label{sec:overhead}
In closed loop, throughput varied by up to $\pm$17\% between identical
untraced runs, so the throughput differences in
Table~\ref{tab:overhead} are within noise; untraced kv was even the
slowest. Tracer CPU per request is the
stable measure. Shared counters cost \ovNsCpuShm{}\,ns (cpu) and
\ovNsKvShm{}\,ns (kv) per request, about as much as the counters-only
baseline. Uprobe windows cost \ovNsKvAttrib--\ovNsCpuAttrib{}\,ns
\emph{before} the cost of the traps themselves, and per-event streaming
costs up to \ovNsCpuStream{}\,ns: 47--53$\times$ and 63--104$\times$ the
cost of shared counters.
Streaming lost no events at about 400k events/s with a 16\,MiB ring
buffer, but it needs an offline join. In open loop, the interleaved A/B
test (\abRuns{} runs per side, kv mid) found no measurable effect: median
p50 \abPfiftyNone{} vs.\ \abPfiftyShm{}\,$\mu$s ($p{=}\abPfiftyP$), median
p99 \abPninetyNone{} vs.\ \abPninetyShm{} ($p{=}\abPninetyP$), and
\abCollNone{} vs.\ \abCollShm{} collapse runs (Fisher $p{=}\abCollP$).
The 5\% budget is therefore met, within the resolution this VM allows.

\subsection{Tail detection (H1)}
\label{sec:h1}
Table~\ref{tab:detect} and Fig.~\ref{fig:pr} show a ceiling and three
findings. The ceiling: \noSigTest\% of the test tails (\noSigHeld\% on net)
carry no OS-delay signal at all, meaning no own or inherited scheduler,
IRQ or blocking delay of 10\,$\mu$s or more. Their delay is pure queueing
or host time the guest cannot see, so no kernel-feature model can rank
them. This is the precision cliff near 0.45 recall in Fig.~\ref{fig:pr}.
\textbf{(1) Queueing dominates detection on the training workloads.} The
queue-length control alone reaches PR-AUC \apQLTest{}, more than any
kernel feature set. Once a request is queued behind others, its fate is
largely decided. The control counts requests that completed \emph{during}
the wait, so it is only known afterwards: it is a diagnostic reference for
how much of the tail is queueing, not a predictor. \textbf{(2) On those workloads, aligned telemetry
is not better than aggregate counts}: \apAlFTest{} vs.\ \apAgFTest{} for
the forest (\apAlLTest{} vs.\ \apAgLTest{} logistic), and 5\,ms aggregates
reach \ablAggFiveTest{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}). In-distribution, H1 is
not supported. \textbf{(3) Aligned telemetry generalizes better.} On the never-seen net
workload (base rate \baseHeld\%), aligned features reach about twice the
PR-AUC of aggregate counts: \apAlFHeld{} (forest), \apAlLHeld{} (logistic)
and \apRuleHeld{} (rule), against \apAgFHeld, \apAgLHeld{} and
\apAggRuleHeld. The aggregates still carry signal, at 4--5$\times$ the
base rate, but transfer worse. The interpretable rule
(``total OS delay above \ruleTau$\times$ the tail threshold'') transfers
as well as the learned models. On top of the queue control, aligned
features also add more on net than aggregates do (logistic \apQAlLHeld{}
vs.\ \apQAgLHeld, forest \apQAlFHeld{} vs.\ \apQAgFHeld; control alone
\apQLHeld), although the confidence intervals overlap. H1 therefore holds
for generalization only.

The ablation explains why. Without \emph{inherited} delay, held-out PR-AUC
drops from \ablAllHeld{} to \ablNoInhHeld{}: most tail requests were
delayed by OS events that happened to the requests ahead of them, not to
themselves. Scheduler features carry the most information (without them:
\ablNoSchedHeld); interrupt and blocking features add little on their own.
Restricting the evaluation to runs with $\le$3\% noise changes no forest
PR-AUC by more than 0.02.

\textbf{Why net is hard.} Net tails are caused on the client side.
Splitting their latency shows that the response path
($t_\mathrm{done}-t_\mathrm{end}$) dominates: the client's receive thread
shares CPU~3 with the spinning generator and waits for its time slice.
Moving the receiver to CPU~2 cut the response-path p99 from
\rcvGenRespP{} to \rcvOwnRespP. Total p99 did not change, though
(\rcvGenP{} vs.\ \rcvOwnP), because the server-side p99 rose to
\rcvOwnServer{}: the receiver now competes with a worker. The delay moves
with the co-located thread, which is an OS-induced tail on a thread
outside the request window. This is the attribution gap that per-request
windows leave open.

\textbf{Early warning.} At the window level (10\,ms), a logistic model on
the telemetry of window $t$ predicts a tail episode in window $t{+}1$ with
PR-AUC \ewApTest{} (test) and \ewApHeld{} (net), above simply repeating
the current state (\ewPersTest, \ewPersHeld).

\subsection{Interventions (H2)}
\label{sec:h2}
Fig.~\ref{fig:signature} gives each intervention's effect on each delay
type. \textbf{Seven of nine predictions hold} (one-sided Mann--Whitney,
5 vs.\ 5 runs, $p\le0.048$):
\begin{itemize}
\item The CPU hog raises mean preemption wait per request
\sigCpuHogRunq$\times$ (cpu, \absCpuHogRunqCtl$\to$\absCpuHogRunqInt\,$\mu$s),
\sigKvHogRunq$\times$ (kv, \absKvHogRunqCtl$\to$\absKvHogRunqInt\,$\mu$s) and
\sigNetHogRunq$\times$ (net, \absNetHogRunqCtl$\to$\absNetHogRunqInt\,$\mu$s),
and wakeup delay 15--46$\times$ (e.g.\ cpu \absCpuHogWakeCtl$\to$\absCpuHogWakeInt\,$\mu$s).
The ratios are large because the baseline is tens of nanoseconds. IRQ time
and blocking do not change.
\item Moving the workers off the hog cuts preemption wait to
\sigCpuMoveRunq$\times$ (cpu, \absCpuMoveRunqCtl$\to$\absCpuMoveRunqInt\,$\mu$s) and
\sigKvMoveRunq$\times$ (kv, \absKvMoveRunqCtl$\to$\absKvMoveRunqInt\,$\mu$s). This is the
reverse effect.
\item The I/O writer raises kv blocking \sigKvIoBlk$\times$
(\absKvIoBlkCtl$\to$\absKvIoBlkInt\,$\mu$s) and IRQ time \sigKvIoIrq$\times$.
\end{itemize}
The two misses are informative. The softirq flood had no significant
effect on kv (\sigKvFloodIrq$\times$ IRQ time): kv workers are often
asleep, so steered softirqs mostly land on idle CPUs. For the cpu workload,
which never blocks, the I/O writer showed up as \sigCpuIoIrq$\times$
\emph{interrupt} time instead (\absCpuIoIrqCtl$\to$\absCpuIoIrqInt\,$\mu$s), because block-completion IRQs land on the
worker CPUs. That is a cause our prediction (blocked) did not anticipate,
and one that only per-thread IRQ charging can reveal.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
All results come from one 4-vCPU VM, one host and one kernel (6.8). Host
preemption and slowdown of vCPUs are invisible to the guest. The noise gate
bounds them, but they remain the likely source of unattributed collapse
episodes. The workloads are synthetic. The inheritance rule assumes a
shared FIFO queue and does not model per-socket queues (net). Test sets
include stressed runs, which raises their tail rate (base rate 20\%).
Shared counters need two calls in the application; unmodified binaries must
use uprobe windows.
