Slow requests in latency-critical services are often slowed by the
operating system rather than the application, yet standard eBPF tools only
report aggregate scheduler and interrupt statistics. We present \sys{}, an
eBPF pipeline that keeps per-thread preemption, wakeup, blocking and
interrupt accounting in an mmap-able BPF map. The application snapshots it
at request boundaries with plain memory loads, with no trap or ring-buffer
record per request. \sys{} costs \ovNsKvShm--\ovNsCpuShm{}\,ns of tracer
CPU per request, 47--53$\times$ less than uprobe-based windows, and closes
an accounting identity within 5\,$\mu$s for at least \valWithin\% of
requests. Across 336 runs of three workloads, a queue-length control
explains most tails, and aligned kernel features detect them no better than
aggregate counts. Their advantage on one held-out workload (PR-AUC
\apAlLHeld{} vs.\ \apAgLHeld) did not survive rotating the held-out
workload or a fresh replication: they only see tails caused inside the
request window. Most of their signal is OS delay \emph{inherited} from
requests queued ahead. One-at-a-time interventions confirm \hTwoRaw{} of
\hTwoN{} pre-stated signatures (\hTwoHolm{} after Holm correction), and
the misses expose an interrupt path we had not predicted. We also show how
hypervisor noise distorts VM-based tail studies, and prepare a
\texttt{runqlat} patch for bcc.
